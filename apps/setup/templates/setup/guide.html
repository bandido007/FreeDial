1. Start here: the order of setting up

Each step uses what the step before it made, so this order saves going back:

  1. People: a user for everyone who signs in, and their roles.
  2. Skills: the languages callers speak and the topics agents know.
  3. Agents: a number and a name for each person who takes calls, with their skills.
  4. Wrap-up codes and Not Ready reasons: what agents pick on their page.
  5. Queues: one per line of business, with its agents. Add skill rule sets only if a queue needs them.
  6. Entry numbers: the numbers callers dial and the queue each one goes to.
  7. The AI agent (optional): who answers first on some numbers.
  8. Teams: which supervisor looks after which agents.
  9. Phones (optional): desk phones and test phones.
  10. Test, until every item of the checklist says Done or Not used.

Every page in the left menu lists what exists; Add makes a new one. Fields in bold are required, and the small grey text under a field explains it. If something you type is not allowed, the page says why next to the field and nothing is saved.

2. The running services

AlgoDial is several programs working together. On the lab machine make up starts the database and Redis, and make dev starts the rest:

ServiceWhat it doesIf it stops
WebThis admin, the agent page and the supervisor page.Nobody can sign in.
BrainDecides every call: which queue, which agent, transfers, supervision.Asterisk sends callers to its backup queue (phones 1001 and 1002 ring). Agent pages do not get calls.
WriterKeeps the call records that the Calls pages and reports read.Calls still work; their records arrive when it starts again.
Live relayPushes changes to the agent and supervisor pages.Pages stop updating until they reconnect.
RecordingsCollects each call's recording after it ends.Recordings arrive later.
AI workerSpeaks for the AI agent.After a few seconds, callers on AI numbers go to a person.

The first section of the checklist shows whether the brain, the writer and the AI worker are running.

3. People: users

Everyone signs in with their email address and a password: agents on the agent page, supervisors on the supervisor page, managers here. To add a person:

  1. Open Users → Add user. Fill in their email, first name, last name, choose Account type: Internal, and type a password twice. Save.
  2. Their page opens. Tick Is verified. Without it the sign-in is refused with "Please verify your email address".
  3. Leave Must change password unticked for agents and supervisors: their pages cannot set a new password yet. Give them their password in person.
  4. Tick Staff status only for people who work in this admin (managers, administrators). Agents and supervisors do not need it.
  5. Leave Superuser status for one or two system owners: it allows everything.
  6. Save, then give them a role (next section).

When someone leaves, open their user and untick Active instead of deleting it: their calls and history stay, and they can no longer sign in.

4. Roles: what each person may do

A role is a set of permissions. To give one, open Roles and permissions → User roles → Add, choose the user and the role, and save. A person can hold several roles; they may do everything any of their roles allows.

  • An agent needs the Agent role, and an agent record (section 6).
  • A supervisor needs the Supervisor role, and a team (section 12).
  • A manager who sets up the centre needs Contact Centre Manager and Staff status.
  • Whoever looks after phones needs Telephony Administrator; whoever adds people needs User Administrator.

5. Skills: languages and topics

A skill is something an agent can do, with a level from 1 to 10. Queues use skills to choose who takes a call (section 9). Open Agents → Skills → Add:

  • Name: one lower-case word, used in rules: swahili, english, claims, tier2.
  • Kind: language (a language callers speak), topic (what the call is about) or tier (seniority).

Use the same meaning for levels everywhere:

LevelMeans
1 to 3Trainee: simple cases, with help
4 to 6Competent: normal cases alone
7 to 8Experienced: hard cases
9 to 10Expert: anything, and coaches others

A centre with one language and no specialities can skip skills: then every agent of a queue takes every call.

6. Agents

An agent is a person who takes calls. Open Agents → Agents → Add:

FieldWhat to put
Number2 to 20 digits, different for each agent. The form is already filled with the next free number of the agent number range (2000 to 6999 unless changed in System settings, "Agent numbers"); keep it, or type another. A number already used, even by a deleted agent, is refused or skipped. It shows on the agent page, the supervisor page and in reports. It is not a number anyone dials: callers dial an entry number.
NameAs colleagues and supervisors should see it (the transfer list shows it).
UserThe person who signs in as this agent (section 3). One person, one agent. Left empty, nobody can sign in as it: only the automatic call tests use such agents.
PhoneEmpty (the usual): calls ring the agent page in the browser. Or choose a desk phone or softphone (a SIP account, section 13): calls ring that phone instead. The agent still signs in on the agent page, which shows the caller and the AI's summary and does Hold, Transfer, Ready and wrap-up; they pick up, hang up, mute and dial on the phone. Two agents may share a phone across shifts, but only one can be signed in on it.
ActiveUntick to stop offering calls to this agent while keeping their history.
Skills (rows below)Each skill they have and its level, e.g. swahili 9, claims 6.

Then put the agent in at least one queue (section 8) and in a team (section 12). The checklist shows any agent who is missing either.

The agent's phone (when Phone is empty) is in their browser: there is no phone account to make. When they sign in at /agent/, AlgoDial switches on a phone account named web + their number (web8003) with a new password, and switches it off when they sign out. Agents → Agent sessions shows who signed in, when, and on which phone.

7. Wrap-up codes and Not Ready reasons

  • Wrap-up codes: after each call the agent picks one to say how it ended (Sale, Complaint, Information given…). Keep the list short and the words plain: reports count them.
  • Not Ready reasons: why an agent is not taking calls (Break, Lunch, Meeting…). Max seconds makes the supervisor page flag an agent who stays longer (e.g. 900 = 15 minutes; 0 = no limit).

Untick Active to retire one; past calls keep the name they were given.

8. Queues

A queue is one line of business (Support, Claims…): who may take its calls, whom it rings first, and what happens when nobody answers. Open Queues → Queues → Add:

FieldWhat it means
NameAs reports and the supervisor page show it.
StrategyWhich free agent gets the next call: Longest idle (fair; the usual choice), Best skilled (highest levels in the skills the call needs), Ring all (every free agent at once; the first to answer takes it), Round robin (in turn), Linear (always from the first member), Fewest calls answered, or Random.
Ring timeoutSeconds an agent's phone rings. An agent who does not answer becomes "Not responding" and the call moves on.
Retry delaySeconds before an agent who refused a call is offered it again.
Max wait / Max callersWhen a caller has waited this long, or this many callers already wait (0 = no limit), the overflow action happens.
Overflow actionAnother queue (choose it in Overflow queue), Voicemail, then a callback task (the caller leaves a message; it appears in Calls → Callback tasks) or Hang up.
PriorityFor an agent in several queues: the caller of the higher-priority queue is offered first.
Wrap-up secondsTime the agent has after a call to choose a code and write notes (0 = none).
Music on hold classWhat waiting callers hear. Leave default unless your telephony administrator added another.
Skill rule setEmpty: every member may take every call. Otherwise see section 9.
Service levelYour target, e.g. 80% of calls answered within 20 seconds. Reports measure each queue against it.
Members (rows below)The agents of this queue. Priority 1 is offered first (it matters for Linear).

An agent can be in several queues. A queue with nobody in it makes callers wait, then overflow: the checklist warns about that.

9. Skill rule sets

A rule set says who may take a queue's call, and loosens as the caller waits: first the best match, then a wider circle, so nobody waits forever for one expert. Open Queues → Skill rule sets → Add. Each row is one minimum level; rows with the same Until seconds are one step. For example:

Until secondsSkillMin levelMeans
30$language6For the first 30 s: a good speaker of the caller's language who knows claims well
30claims7
(empty)$language4After that, for as long as it takes: anyone who speaks it and knows claims a little
(empty)claims1
  • $language means the caller's language (from the entry number or the AI). Every step must ask for it at 4 or more: a caller is never given to someone who cannot speak with them.
  • Only the last step has empty seconds (it lasts until the call is answered or overflows); the seconds of the other steps grow from step to step.
  • The page draws the steps as you type (Routing steps), and Test with agent tells you whether a given agent would match at a given wait.

Then choose the rule set on the queue. The checklist warns when no member of a queue meets its last step.

10. Entry numbers

An entry number is what callers dial. Open Queues → Entry points → Add:

FieldWhat it means
Number800 to 899 for numbers dialled inside the centre (Asterisk hands only these to AlgoDial), or a DID: a number from a carrier, written in E.164 (+255222123456). See "Carriers" below.
NameA label for you and for reports.
QueueWhere callers go.
AI agentOptional: the AI answers first and hands the caller to a person (section 11). Empty: straight to the queue.
LanguageThe callers' language until something better is known. It decides the AI's greeting and $language in skill rules.
PriorityEmpty: the queue's priority. Set it higher to serve this number's callers first.
Fallback queueWhere the call goes if the AI or a routing decision fails.

On the lab: 800 goes to Support; 801 to 803 are the demo helpline (802 and 803 are answered by Jambo AI first); 897 and 899 are used by the automatic call tests. A number that is not an entry point is hung up at once.

11. The AI agent

The AI agent answers a call, helps with what it may answer, and hands the caller to a person with a short summary that the agent sees before answering. Open AI agents:

  • Name: what it calls itself, e.g. Jambo AI.
  • Instructions: who the helpline is, opening hours, what it may answer and what must go to a person. It says nothing that is not written here.
  • Greetings: its first words in Swahili and in English (chosen by the entry number's language).
  • When a person takes over: after this many turns it did not understand; at once for any of the sensitive topics (one per line); and if it is not on the line within start timeout seconds. It also hands over whenever the caller asks for a person.
  • Voice and models: already set to the ones that sounded best in our tests. Change them only on advice.
  • Where it may send callers (rows below): each queue it may choose, and when, in plain words ("a specific claim or payment"). It may choose only these.

Then choose the AI agent on an entry number. It needs the OpenAI key (OPENAI_API_KEY in the server's .env file) and the AI worker running; the checklist checks both. Each call's page shows what the AI and the caller said.

12. Teams and supervisors

A supervisor sees, listens to and helps only the agents of their teams. Open Teams → Teams → Add: a name, then move people into Supervisors and Agents. The lists are users (people), not agent numbers. Nobody can be a supervisor and an agent of the same team.

The supervisor signs in at /supervisor/, sees who waits and who is on a call, and can monitor (listen), whisper (only the agent hears) or barge (everyone hears), and change an agent's state.

13. Phones (SIP accounts)

Agents need nothing here: their phone is in the browser (section 6). Make a SIP account for a desk phone, a softphone such as Zoiper, or a test phone. Open Telephony → SIP accounts → Add:

  • SIP id: the phone's number and username, e.g. 1003. Ids starting with web are refused: they belong to the browser phones AlgoDial makes at sign-in (web2468 for agent 2468).
  • Kind: SOFTPHONE, the only choice when adding. BROWSER accounts (web…) are made, given a new password and switched on and off by AlgoDial: do not edit them.
  • Display name: the name the other side sees.
  • New password: at least 12 characters, or leave it empty and AlgoDial makes one. It is shown once, at the top of the page after saving: type it into the phone straight away.
  • Codecs and Context: leave empty.

In the phone (Zoiper: Settings → Accounts → Add, SIP): Domain = this server's address with port 5062 (on the lab laptop itself 127.0.0.1:5062; from a phone on the same Wi-Fi, the laptop's IP address, e.g. 192.168.1.20:5062), Username = the SIP id, Password as above, transport UDP. It should show Registered. On the lab, phones 1000 to 1009 ring each other by dialling the number, and dial 800 to call the centre.

To let an agent take calls on this phone, open the agent (section 6) and choose it as their Phone. At sign-in the agent page checks that the phone is registered; if not, it says so and the agent cannot go Ready until it is.

To change a password, type a new one in New password, save, and type it in the phone too. Untick Active to stop a phone at once.

Carriers: trunks, DIDs and outbound routes

A trunk connects AlgoDial to a carrier, so the public can call in and agents can transfer calls out. Open Telephony → Trunks → Add and start from a preset:

  • Name: lower-case, e.g. vodacom. Its Asterisk endpoint is trunk-vodacom.
  • Style: IP (the carrier sends and takes calls by address) or Registration (AlgoDial signs in with the carrier's username and password).
  • Hosts: the carrier's IP addresses (with :port if not 5060). Only these addresses may use the trunk.
  • Max channels: calls in and out at once; more are refused (0 = no limit).
  • Numbers in (country code, national and international prefix, national length): how the carrier writes numbers, so every form becomes one E.164 number.
  • Numbers out (strip digits, prefix): what the carrier wants. E.164 as it is: 0 and empty. National: strip 4 (+255), prefix 0. External prefix: what agents may type first for an outside line (e.g. 9).

A DID is a number a carrier sends you. Make it an entry point in E.164 (+255222123456): it gets its queue, language, AI agent and priority like any entry number. Calls to numbers that are not DIDs are refused by Asterisk itself, even when AlgoDial is restarting.

An outbound route says which outside numbers may be called, and through which trunks (Telephony → Outbound routes → Add):

  • Patterns: one E.164 prefix per line, e.g. +2557 and +2556 for mobiles. Anything no route names is refused: other countries need a route naming them (+254), and premium numbers (System settings → Phone numbers) a route naming the premium prefix.
  • Trunks, in order: the first is tried first. The next is tried only when the carrier or the network fails, never when the person is busy, does not answer or refuses.
  • Caller ID: the number the person called sees.
  • Required permission: agents need it to call these numbers (Call outside numbers, can_dial_external, given through a role).

Agents reach an outside number with Transfer → Number, typing it as they would on a phone (0712 345 678).

14. Testing that it works

Do this after setting up, and again after a big change. Use two browsers (or a normal and a private window) so the agent and the supervisor are signed in at the same time.

  1. Fix everything the checklist marks Needs attention.
  2. Sign in at /agent/ as an agent, allow the microphone, press Go Ready. The bar turns green.
  3. From a desk phone, call your entry number (on the lab: 800). Answer on the agent page. Talk both ways; try Hold and Resume.
  4. Transfer the call to a colleague who is Ready, or hang up. Choose a wrap-up code and finish.
  5. As a supervisor at /supervisor/, during another call press Monitor, then Whisper.
  6. If you use the AI: call an AI number, ask a question, then ask for a person. The agent sees the AI's summary.
  7. Here in the admin, open Calls: your calls are listed, the recording plays, and Reports → Today counts them.
  8. Come back to this page. The automatic tests tick themselves; confirm the hands-on ones with Confirm it works.

For timed tests with real agents, see the manual test script (docs/MANUAL_TEST_SCRIPT.md).

15. Every day: calls, callbacks, reports

  • Calls: every call, with who answered, how it ended, the AI's part, transfers and the recording (left ear: the caller; right ear: what they heard). Playing recordings needs the permission to listen to recordings.
  • Callback tasks: voicemails left when a queue overflowed. Listen, call the person back, then mark it Called back.
  • Reports: calls per queue (service level, abandoned calls, waiting and handling times), agents' time in each state, what the AI handled, and how well AlgoDial works for its users (how fast offers are answered, wrap-up time, transfer success, how quickly supervisors act on long breaks, whether buttons answer within their 1-second budget), for any period, as PDF, Excel or CSV.
  • Agent sessions: who signed in, when, on which phone.
  • Audit logs (Audit & system logs): who changed what in this admin, and when.

16. When you change something

A save takes effect at once: AlgoDial picks up queues, agents, entry numbers and AI settings without a restart. New calls follow the new settings; calls already in progress carry on. Every change is kept in the audit log with your name.

Changes to Asterisk itself (which numbers reach AlgoDial, recordings, the backup queue) are made by the telephony administrator on the server, not here.
